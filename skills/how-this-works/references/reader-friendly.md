# Explain a project to a new reader

A reader should understand the project without knowing the researcher's background or opening every source link. Let the materials determine emphasis; directory size and file type do not determine importance.

## Start with the project's value

Explain who uses it, the problem they face, what they provide, and what they get. Make the main capabilities recognizable before developing a representative example. Scenario titles describe a task the project helps accomplish; the example in the body makes its choices concrete without turning the project's identity into that one example.

Choose scenarios for distinct user tasks or important choices, not to satisfy a count of units or directories. Keep independently important capabilities reachable, even when they are outside the introductory scenario. A second capability may need another scenario or a clear responsibility entry, not equal space everywhere.

Learning order may differ from execution order. State a premise such as “the rules are already loaded,” explain the useful task, then explore how the premise is established. Include preparation where it changes a key behavior or failure condition; otherwise installation, distribution, compatibility, and maintenance can remain in the relevant responsibility. Do not make all implementation preparation a prerequisite course.

Use the homepage for purpose and scenario choices. The architecture graph helps readers generalize after a scenario and compare the participating responsibilities. A responsibility explains its own mechanisms; its explanations must also make sense when opened directly. Preserve the originating scenario, step, and return position using the existing interactions. Do not create a duplicate “project overview” entry.

## Develop the reasoning

A summary helps a reader choose an entry. Expanding it should add a reason, condition, intermediate change, or example. Explain why a choice fits, what it costs, and which changed conditions would call for another approach. Lists of functions or rules cannot replace that reasoning.

Use a supported concrete input to connect what each step receives, changes, and returns. Keep purpose and method together rather than separating them behind different clicks. Distinguish work done by the project, caller, and Agent. Label illustrations; an operational walkthrough needs supported entry points, compatible step conditions, and a way to recognize the result.

Develop an example only as far as needed to explain the project's approach and its limits. If substituting another tool would leave most of the prose unchanged while this project's mechanism remains unclear, the example has taken over. Avoid repeating it across the introduction, example box, and scenario.

Let each paragraph develop one understanding. Introduce terms when needed and explain their role, not merely a translation: “an isolated environment” still needs a reason for isolation; a “score” needs an explanation of what verification measured. Function names and citations locate evidence but do not fill reasoning gaps. Use as much prose as understanding requires, without word quotas or a fixed sentence pattern.

## Put the explanation where readers need it

Use the [input contract](current-model.md) to associate explanations with real responsibilities, scenarios, and evidence. Preserve facts, reasons, conditions, and examples when adapting prose; splitting, merging, and rewriting are allowed. Do not mechanically turn paragraphs into nodes or compress a complete explanation into a navigation label.

Titles should say what a reader will learn. “Choose tasks and an assistant to evaluate” describes a purpose; “Construct JobConfig” only names an operation. Introduce implementation objects when locating the mechanism. Put short labels on relationships and their conditions in adjacent prose; the endpoints must represent the parties actually cooperating.

Supply context for direct entry. Replace “cancel the previous wait” with “when new input arrives, cancel the previous wait if it has not finished.” Do not require the previous scenario step to explain an isolated passage. Preserve necessary background without repeating the whole scenario or inventing behavior.

Use `parent` for real composition, never to hide secondary content under an invented owner. Several implementations of one responsibility can be separate explanations rather than top-level nodes. Features, steps, responsibilities, and methods do not need a uniform hierarchy. Keep source material in the inventory and evidence links; retain the understanding graph and its surrounding context.

Reuse established understanding and evidence. Read gaps that affect the explanation rather than rereading the repository to adapt a page. Final prose, evidence, and scope stay in the existing study; a draft is optional. Scripts generate source entry points from paths and ranges. Logical index paths are not automatically accessible filesystem links, and text coverage does not prove readable or accurate explanations.

## Preserve evidence and boundaries

Distinguish author requirements, implementation facts, inferences, and execution observations near the claims they support. See [Research judgments](research-contract.md) for evidence and scope decisions. Summaries and scenarios must retain conditions and exceptions from the detailed evidence; shared names or rule sources do not establish identical behavior across entry points.

A boundary should help the reader decide what to verify next. Avoid surrounding every statement with the same disclaimer. State the conditions of performance figures and when reused experiments occurred. Preserve unexamined material as a gap without letting disclaimers replace missing core explanations.

History should explain the old problem, why the earlier approach made sense, later limitations, and the changes and costs of its replacement. Keep unknown reasons, accidents, and compatibility burdens visible. Read available full commit messages before calling motivation unknown; distinguish author reasons from inferences. Preserve superseded judgments and old evidence. A responsibility's deletion does not prove that its problem disappeared or that a later replacement is equivalent.

## Read back the result

As part of writing, follow the final landing page through a core scenario to an expanded explanation, and open a responsibility directly. Use the webpage or `materials.mjs overview`, `scenario`, and `unit` against the delivered `agent/` index; these reader commands are not `study.mjs` commands.

Look for missing major capabilities, unexplained terms, repeated summaries, disconnected reasons, and supporting details that crowd out the task. Check that a reader can follow the actual step conditions, reach the responsible parts and evidence, and distinguish what is known from what remains open. Revise the same study when needed; do not add a separate spot-check, scoring, or independent review workflow.

Keep concrete findings in the research artifacts. Self-review records a reading check, not proof that users mastered the project. Browser checks establish presentation and interaction, not semantic truth.
